How it works

You describe music to your AI client (Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity). It uses the midimcp tools to write notes into a song stored on your NAS. This site plays, adjusts and exports those songs. Every change is saved as a new revision, so nothing is ever lost.

Installing on TrueNAS or Docker, connecting AI clients, updating, or building and publishing the image yourself: see Setup.

Each recipe below has a Copy button. Paste it into your AI client and change the words in [brackets].

Your first song

Using the midimcp tools, read the midimcp://guide resource first. Then create an original 16-bar loopable [medieval town] theme in the character of late-1990s fantasy RPG MIDI music: [wooden flute] melody, [lute] accompaniment, no percussion, [G#] [Ionian], [84] BPM, [6/8]. Validate it and render a preview.

Good for a quick sketch. For fuller arrangements, start from a template (next section).

Songs from templates

Templates give the AI a plan: which instruments play which role, their pitch ranges, the song form (A / B / A′…) and a chord for every bar. Browse them on the Templates tab, where each one also has a ready-made prompt.

Built-in templates: peaceful-town, forest-exploration, ruined-crypt, tavern, sea-voyage, tense-borderlands, plus the Old Realm and retro game families.

Using midimcp, create a song from the "[peaceful-town]" template titled "[Lanterns of the Vale]" in [G#]. Fill every section of every track following the chord chart and the template guidance. Then validate, fix the warnings that make sense, and render a preview.

Override anything the template sets:

Using midimcp, create a song from the "[forest-exploration]" template titled "[Mossway]", but in [E] [aeolian], at [72] BPM, [48] bars long. Keep the template's instruments and form. Fill every section, validate, render a preview.

Your own templates: on the Templates tab, open a built-in one, click Duplicate, and edit the copy, e.g. swap the cello for a harp, change the chord progressions or the section lengths. Then ask for a song from your template's id.

Building a richer song step by step

AI agents usually write better music in several focused turns than in one giant request. A sequence that works well (send one message at a time, listen in between):

  1. Using midimcp, create a song from the "[sea-voyage]" template titled "[Westward Sails]". Don't write notes yet: show me the tracks, sections and chord chart you got.
  2. Write only the melody for section A (bars 1-8) following the chords: a memorable 2-bar motif, developed over two 4-bar phrases, breathing at bar 4 and 8. Render a preview of bars 1-8.
  3. Now write the accompaniment and bass for section A following the template's patterns, keeping everything below the melody. Render bars 1-8 again.
  4. Write section B as a contrast: melody higher with longer notes, the countermelody answering in the gaps, pad swelling. Keep section A unchanged. Render bars 9-16.
  5. Write A' as a varied return of the A theme with a fuller accompaniment, then the last section so it leads smoothly back to bar 1. Validate the whole song and fix the musical warnings, especially loop warnings.
  6. Give the melody more life: vary velocities to shape each phrase (softer at phrase starts, peak in the middle), add one or two passing notes per phrase. Don't change the accompaniment.

Tip: tell the AI what you heard. "The flute is too busy in bars 5-6" works better than "make it better".

Making it sound human

A melody typed exactly on the beat, every note equally loud and with nothing happening inside held notes sounds robotic. Checks warns MECHANICAL_MELODY when that happens. Three tools fix it. They never change pitches, and they are repeatable: the same request gives the same result.

  • Humanize: tiny timing pushes (bar downbeats stay exact), phrase-shaped loudness, accents, smooth or detached notes, breaths.
  • Expression: the volume swells across each phrase and inside long notes; a gentle vibrato fades in on held notes. Best for flute, recorder, oboe, strings, horn and voices; plucked and struck instruments don't need it.
  • Automation: hand-made gestures such as a fade-in, a slide into a note, or a vibrato swell on the last note.
In "[Lanterns of the Vale]", make the [flute] sound played instead of typed: humanize it (legato 0.92, breaths of about 180 ticks between phrases, subtle timing), then shape its expression with phrase swells and a gentle delayed vibrato on held notes. Humanize the [lute] lightly (timing only). Leave pitches unchanged, validate and render bars 1-8.
In "[Lanterns of the Vale]", before humanizing, give the [flute] melody more life as a composer would: shape velocities per phrase, add a grace note or mordent on 2-3 long notes per phrase and a short trill before each cadence. Keep the tune recognisable.
In "[Lanterns of the Vale]", fade the [strings] in over the first 2 bars (expression 40 to 110, ramped) and add a slow vibrato swell on the [flute]'s final note.

Run humanize once, after the notes are final. Not right? Ask for "the same with seed 2" for a different take, or "less timing variation".

Retro and Old Realm styles

The templates describe a style (instruments, tempo, form, chords, playing patterns), never a melody, and every one tells the AI not to copy existing games. Use them for music in the spirit of an era.

  • Old Realm, in the spirit of late-90s online fantasy RPGs: old-realm-title (login screen), old-realm-town, old-realm-forest, old-realm-sailing, old-realm-dungeon, old-realm-battle, old-realm-castle, old-realm-shrine, old-realm-wilderness.
  • Retro games: retro-8bit-overworld, retro-8bit-dungeon (chip-style: every part one note at a time), retro-16bit-jrpg-town, retro-16bit-jrpg-battle, retro-16bit-boss, retro-16bit-world-map, retro-platformer-stage, retro-puzzle-game, retro-space-shooter, retro-gothic-castle, retro-pc-adventure (early PC sound-card style), retro-victory-fanfare (short jingle, no loop).

For each: fill every section following the chord chart, follow the template's Expression advice, validate and render a preview. Original melodies only.

Editing parts of a song

In "[Lanterns of the Vale]", simplify the flute in bars [9-16]; leave every other track untouched.
In "[Lanterns of the Vale]", add a [harp] track playing gentle arpeggios in section B only, below the flute.
In "[Lanterns of the Vale]", restore revision [7], then redo only the B section melody with a calmer contour.

Protecting parts: a locked track or bar range can't be changed by the AI. Only you can set locks (on purpose, the AI can't lock or unlock); the lock controls arrive in the web UI with the piano-roll feature. Until then, say "don't touch the lute" in your prompt.

Instruments, volume, mute: change them on the Songs page. Each change is a revision, and the ↺ button puts a setting back to its default.

Making loops sound good

Game music repeats, so the joint between the end and the start matters. Turn on Loop on the Songs page: it plays exactly the loop section back-to-back with no gap, as a game would.

  • Harmony: the last bar should lead back to the first chord (V→I, bVII→i). Templates already do this.
  • Melody: the last note should be a step or small leap from the first note.
  • No cut-offs: notes must end at the loop point, or they get chopped on the restart.
  • Same energy: the last bar should be about as busy as the first; avoid a quiet "ending".
Check "[Lanterns of the Vale]" for loop problems (validate_project loop warnings) and fix them so the last bar flows seamlessly into bar 1, without changing bars 1-24.

Downloading

Open a song and click ⬇ Download song at the top. The list next to it picks the format: MP3 (320, 192 or 128 kbps), WAV (lossless), MIDI (for games and music editors) or Project JSON (backup). Your choice is remembered. The Download card further down has one button per format, and lists earlier downloads, which stay in the song's exports folder on the NAS. You can also ask:

Export "[Lanterns of the Vale]" as mp3 at 320 kbps and as MIDI, and give me the download paths.

Using this page

  • Speed (50–150 %) is preview-only: the song is re-rendered at that tempo, pitch unchanged.
  • Overview: click anywhere to jump there.
  • Checks lists errors (must fix) and warnings (advice; your call).
  • Favorites and collections: click ☆ next to a song (or its title) to star it. + Collection makes your own category (e.g. "Town themes", "Boss fights"); open a song and tick collections under its title. The chips above the list filter by All / ★ Favorites / a collection; with a collection selected you can rename or delete it (its songs are kept). The list sorts by newest, A–Z, or favorites first. A song can be in several collections. Your AI client can file a song into an existing collection: "add it to my Town themes".
  • Delete song moves it to trash/ on the NAS; move the folder back into projects/ to restore it.
  • Edits made by the AI appear live; while playing you get a "Load new version" button instead.

Troubleshooting

  • "That instrument is not on this song's allowed list": the song was created with a fixed instrument list. Pick the instrument from "Other instruments" and confirm, which adds it.
  • REVISION_CONFLICT: someone (you or the AI) changed the song meanwhile; the page reloads the latest version, so try again.
  • The AI asks for permission for every tool: in Claude Code allow all with the permission rule mcp__midimcp; in Codex choose "allow for this session".
  • Nothing to render: the song (or every unmuted track) has no notes yet.
